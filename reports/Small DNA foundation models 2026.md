# Build kron-dna on codons, not genomes

The single most useful finding for kron-dna is that its two nearest neighbours already exist and both succeeded on an academic budget: **HAD**, a bidirectional Gated DeltaNet DNA model of about **1.1M parameters** that beat Caduceus and its own 500M teacher on several histone tasks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20836v1)), and **CodonMamba**, a **71M bidirectional Mamba** coding-sequence model trained on **4× A100 for roughly 7 days** that ranks first on 10 of 12 mRNA benchmarks ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746601v1.full)). Together they bracket the design space exactly: HAD proves the delta-rule layer works on DNA but is tiny, distilled and genome-scale; CodonMamba proves the coding-DNA niche is winnable at kron-dna's compute but uses Mamba, not a delta rule. No one has put a gated delta-rule recurrence on coding DNA, which makes kron-dna genuinely novel rather than a reimplementation. The second decisive finding is that **objective design beats parameter count in the 50–150M range**: the two largest documented single-change wins in codon modelling — HELM's hierarchical codon loss ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12459v2)) and SynCodonLM's synonymous-constrained softmax ([NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/5/gkag166/8496864)) — are free in compute and both portable to a KronLSTM backbone (NX-AI's Kronecker delta-rule layer, [Hartl et al., arXiv:2610.26834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26834)). The third is a warning: **CodonBench showed 98.3% gene overlap between train and test under standard 80/20 splits**, and a model-free (position, codon) memorisation canary beats every neural model on synonymous-variant prediction under those splits ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.12.744371v1.full)). A kron-dna paper that reports gene-held-out results with that canary attached will be more credible than one that reports a higher number without it.

## HAD and CodonMamba are the two papers kron-dna must cite and beat

HAD is the only published DNA foundation model built on Gated DeltaNet. Its student is **4 bidirectional GDN blocks at d=128 plus one FlashAttention layer and a gated MLP**, trained on GRCh38 at sequence length 1,026 with character-level tokens, distilled from **NTv2-500M** using MSE feature alignment on visible positions plus cross-attention reconstruction of masked ones ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20836v1)). Its Genomic Benchmarks average of **0.875** beats Caduceus at 0.866, HyenaDNA at 0.853, Mamba at 0.846 and a CNN at 0.803, and on histone marks it beats a teacher 450× its size (**H3K4me3 0.643 vs 0.410**). It loses on splice sites (**Splice-all 0.911 vs the teacher's 0.983**), which is the consistent failure mode of every tiny recurrent DNA model and reflects local motif sharpness rather than long-range modelling. Crucially, HAD never ablated GDN against Mamba, reports no compute figures, and ships no code — so it establishes that a delta rule is *viable* on DNA, not that it is *better*. That gap is kron-dna's opening. The only other delta-rule biological sequence model found is **ProtSyntax**, a protein model whose "Bi-GDN" fuses two parameter-independent GDN scans by zero-parameter cross-gating and gains **+4.3% MCC** on phosphorylation sites in ablation ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.18.739331v1.full)) — useful as a fusion-design precedent, not as a DNA result. No RWKV or RetNet DNA model exists, which matters because RWKV-7 is itself a generalised delta rule.

CodonMamba is the closer analogue on the data axis. Eight stacked bidirectional Mamba blocks at hidden 768, **~71M trainable parameters**, non-overlapping codon triplets over a 64+5 vocabulary, MLM at 15% masking, **1,024-codon context**, trained for 47 epochs at lr 4e-4 on the **processed CaLM corpus of 8,771,938 coding sequences from 1,544 organisms** (ENA April-2022 snapshot, CD-HIT at 40% amino-acid identity) ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746601v1.full)). Its bidirectional design explicitly follows Caduceus. It wins 10 of 12 benchmarks against DNA, ncRNA and protein models, losing *E. coli* proteins to codonGPT and subcellular localisation to CaLM. It has no experimental validation and no stated software licence. A kron-dna that matches this setup — same corpus, same context, same compute envelope — and swaps BiMamba for a bidirectional KronLSTM produces the **first matched comparison of a delta-rule recurrence against Mamba on biological sequence data**, which no paper in either literature currently provides.

The rest of the field sorts into baselines you should run and recipes you should borrow. The shortlist below is restricted to models that are permissively licensed, runnable on A100s, and size-comparable:

If kron-dna wants a distillation teacher on A100s, GENERator-1.2B (MIT) or NTv2-500M (HAD's own teacher, CC-BY-NC-SA) are the practical choices.

## Two free loss-function changes outperform anything scale buys below 150M

The cleanest matched ablation in codon modelling is HELM's: the same ~50M GPT-2-style setup trained with nucleotide, codon and 6-mer tokenization, scored by Spearman on five datasets ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12459v2)). Codon tokenization wins four of five (Ab1 0.747 vs 0.699 nucleotide; MLOS 0.654 vs 0.528 — the largest gap, **+12.6 points**), but 6-mer beats codon on mRFP (0.857 vs 0.754), so the paper's prose overstates its own table. The more important result is that HELM's real contribution is the **loss**, not the tokenizer: a hierarchical cross-entropy over a codon tree weighted by λ(C) = exp(−α·h(C)) with **α = 0.2**, which penalises synonymous confusions less than cross-amino-acid ones. It **improved MLM on all seven downstream tasks and CLM on five of seven**, lifting mRFP from 0.753 to 0.822 and Tc-riboswitches from 0.569 to 0.626, and it requires no architecture or tokenizer change at all.

SynCodonLM arrives at the same place from a different direction: add a [V,V] codon→synonym matrix to the logits before the softmax so non-synonymous codons get −∞, making the task purely "which synonymous codon, given the protein". That single change let a **102M DeBERTaV2** beat CaLM, CodonBERT, CodonTransformer, cdsBERT and Mistral-Codon on **6 of 7** DNA-level-sensitive datasets at P<.05 ([NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/5/gkag166/8496864)). NVIDIA's third variant on this theme, codon-frequency-weighted masking in EnCodon-1B-CDWT, is more equivocal and the authors say so: it gives the best DDD/ASD case-control separation and the highest KNN purity but is **worse than random-masking models on translation efficiency and mRFP expression**, because its embeddings are less influenced by simple sequence features like GC content ([NVIDIA preprint](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dbr/assets/data/manuscripts/nv-codonfm-preprint.pdf)).

Scale, by comparison, buys little. Within the CodonFM family, translation-efficiency R² runs roughly **0.44 at 80M, 0.49 at 600M and 0.52 at 1B**, and mRFP Spearman **0.655 / 0.71 / 0.73** — so an 80M model already beats CodonBERT and mRNA-FM on both tasks and sits about 0.08 R² behind a model 12× its size ([NVIDIA preprint](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dbr/assets/data/manuscripts/nv-codonfm-preprint.pdf)). The independent view agrees: GENEB probed **40 genomic foundation models on 100 tasks** and found aggregate leaderboards unstable, rankings sharply category-dependent, and that "scale yields only modest and inconsistent gains", with architecture and pretraining alignment often mattering more than parameter count ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04525v4)). **Nobody has run HELM's hierarchical loss against SynCodonLM's constrained softmax on the same corpus and backbone.** That comparison is cheap, uncontested and publishable on its own, and kron-dna is well placed to run it as an ablation rather than a separate paper.

There is one more argument for the coding-DNA choice that is specifically architectural. Roche's wet-lab study found that masking reduced per-amino-acid prediction fidelity below 20% of its unperturbed value at a window of just **17 codons (~51 nt)**, and distal context could not restore the predictions ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.11.744178v1.full)). With CDS averaging **407 codons** in SynCodonLM's corpus, the task's real receptive-field requirement is short. A matrix-state linear RNN therefore gives up almost nothing relative to full attention on coding DNA — a far better-matched claim than "recurrence wins on whole genomes", which the long-range benchmarks do not support.

## A PS wrapper fits a delta-rule layer, but only with a bidirectional objective

Reverse complement handling has two working recipes and neither dominates. Caduceus's **MambaDNA channel split (PS)** takes half the channels through the sequence operator directly and reverse-complements the other half, runs it through *the same shared-weight operator*, and flips it back before concatenation — without doubling the channel count ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03234)). **PH** instead uses RC augmentation during pretraining and averages forward and RC predictions at inference, costing two forward passes. In Caduceus, PH won 5 of 8 Genomic Benchmarks tasks overall, while PS won the enhancer and regulatory tasks and showed its advantage growing with distance to the TSS on eQTL, beating Enformer beyond 100 kb — but PS also had high variance (H3K14ac 0.541 ± 0.212). The construction transfers cleanly: **DNA-xLSTM-PS uses the identical split and beats Caduceus-PS in pretraining loss at both 500k and 2M parameters, with the gap widening as size grows** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04165)).

The wrapper is operator-agnostic, requiring only a map from (B, L, D/2) to (B, L, D/2), so a KronLSTM block with its q/k/v projections, channel-wise decay, erase and write gates and short convolution drops in unchanged. The short causal convolution is fine because the RC'd half sees it flipped by construction. But **PS and a causal objective do not mix**: the RC half's output at position t depends on tokens after t, which leaks the future under next-token prediction. Every published PS model — Caduceus, DNA-xLSTM-PS, PlantCAD — is masked or bidirectional. Two further delta-rule-specific constraints follow from the recurrence itself. Bio-xLSTM can accumulate forward and backward mLSTM states in a single pass because its gates depend only on the current step; **the delta rule's erase term (I − β k kᵀ) makes the state order-dependent, so forward and backward states are genuinely distinct objects and two scans are required**. For long context, Bio-xLSTM's cheaper alternative — alternating the scan direction per block, which costs the same as a causal model and becomes bidirectional after two blocks — is the better fit.

Whatever is chosen, both strands should be averaged at evaluation. **MirrorDNA found NT-v2, DNABERT-2 and HyenaDNA change 25–31% of their predictions when test sequences are reverse-complemented, and a model trained with RC augmentation still flips about 1 in 10 unless outputs are averaged** ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.04.764207v1)). The same paper found BPE and k-mer tokenizers split **92–100% of TF motifs differently on the two strands** — a decisive argument against BPE for any strand-symmetric model, and a reason codon or single-nucleotide tokens are the right default. For a coding-DNA model the issue is partly moot inside the CDS, since the reverse complement of a coding strand is not itself coding; the honest framing is that RC equivariance matters for the genome-wide evaluation tasks kron-dna will also run, not for the CDS pretraining objective.

On the attention question, the general-LM evidence says keep a few attention layers. Gated DeltaNet at 1.3B scores **100/99.8/92.2/29.6 on S-NIAH-2 at 1K/2K/4K/8K** — strong against Mamba-2 and DeltaNet up to 4k, then a collapse at 8k ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06464)). Gated DeltaNet-2's channel-wise erase and write gates improve that to **39.2 vs 32.0 at 8K** but do not fix it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22791v1)). Every scaled DNA hybrid already concedes the point: HybriDNA interleaves Mamba-2 and attention at **7:1** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10807)), StripedHyena 2 uses 5 MHA operators in 32 layers ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01868v1)), HAD uses one attention layer and measured its MLM validation loss improving from **1.0090 to 1.0033** when it was added, and ProtSyntax uses 3:1. One attention or sliding-window layer per 4–8 KronLSTM blocks is the defensible default. StripedHyena 2's one public layout ablation also argues for multi-scale mixing — **SE-MR-LI at PPL 2.83 beat all-long-convolution at 2.87 and all-attention at 3.09** at 7B/400B tokens — which maps onto giving some KronLSTM heads fast-decay initialisation to cover the medium (~100 bp) range that a short conv and a long recurrent state otherwise straddle.

## Sizing: 50–150M parameters on 50–200B codon tokens, four to six A100-weeks

The compute question has an unusually precise answer because three recent coding-DNA models published theirs and all three sit inside kron-dna's budget.

| Model | Params | Hardware | Wall-clock | Tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CodonBERT | 12L/768 | 4× A10G | ~2 weeks | 7 epochs of ~10M CDS |
| cdsFM EnCodon/DeCodon | 80M–1B | 2× H100 | ~2 weeks | >1,000,000 steps |
| SynCodonLM | 102M | 12× L40S | ~8 days | ~100.1B |
| CodonMamba | 71M | 4× A100 | ~7 days | 47 epochs of 8.77M CDS |

Sources: CodonBERT ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.09.09.556981v2.full)); cdsFM ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11482952/)); SynCodonLM ([NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/5/gkag166/8496864)); CodonMamba ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746601v1.full)). NVIDIA published none of these numbers for CodonFM, which is itself a differentiator available to kron-dna for free.

Taking A100 bf16 at 30–45% MFU as roughly 1e19 FLOPs per GPU-day and FLOPs ≈ 6ND, a **100M-parameter model on 100B tokens costs about 6e19 FLOPs, or six GPU-days**; 300M on 100B costs about 18. Six GPUs for two weeks is roughly 8e20 FLOPs in total — about ten runs at the top of the budget used by the only two published DNA scaling sweeps. Those sweeps are themselves the most attractive target. **dnaHNet fit PPL = A·C^−α and measured α = 0.06 for its own architecture, 0.04 for StripedHyena 2 and 0.01 for a Transformer over 4e18–8e19 FLOPs and 10M–1B parameters, with a compute-optimal 218M model trained on 140B tokens at 8e19 FLOPs** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10603v2)). Reproducing that IsoFLOP sweep for KronLSTM at a few GPU-days per point, on the same GTDB-derived corpus, would place a delta-rule exponent on a published curve — a cleaner contribution than another benchmark row, and one no DNA scaling study currently provides for linear-recurrent or delta-rule layers.

On tokens per parameter, DNA practice runs far past Chinchilla's ~20. NT-v2-50M used about 6,000 tokens per parameter, Evo 2 1B about 1,000, ModernGENA roughly 11,000 BPE tokens per parameter over 138–158 epochs ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.21.719816v1.full)), and multi-epoch reuse is clearly safe at these scales. For a 50–150M kron-dna, plan on **50–200B codon tokens** and treat overtraining as cheap insurance. The data exists comfortably: SynCodonLM's **43,526,014 CDS from 35,618 species** is on the Hub with an Apache-2.0 model and a published clustering recipe (**MMseqs2 per species at 90% identity / 80% coverage**), CodonFM's pipeline ships cluster indices built specifically to construct leakage-controlled splits ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA-BioNeMo/CodonFM)), and OpenGenome2 offers 351B tokens of GTDB prokaryotic sequence plus 89–99B of eukaryotic mRNA under Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arcinstitute/opengenome2)). Data quantity is not the constraint; mixture and deduplication are, and **no paper has ablated redundancy-reduction stringency** (CaLM's 40% amino-acid clustering versus SynCodonLM's 90% nucleotide) — another cheap experiment sitting unclaimed.

Two efficiency wins are specific to the recurrent choice. At ~407 codons average against a 1,024–2,048 token context, **no published codon model describes sequence packing**, which wastes most positions; a recurrent model with document-boundary state resets gets that back almost for free. And throughput favours recurrence at length: a 1.3B GDN-2 hybrid holds **38.0 → 36.1 K tokens/s on one H100 as sequence length grows** while a Transformer degrades sharply ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22791v1)), and HybriDNA measured **~3.4× transformer throughput at ~49k context on 4× A100**, where a FlashAttention-2 transformer runs out of memory at ~65k ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10807)).

Concretely: **RefSeq CDS with frame, AUG-start, stop-codon and alphabet filters; MMseqs2 per-species clustering at 90/80 with group-balanced sampling so no clade dominates; a 64+5 codon vocabulary; 1,024-codon context with packing; 15% masking plus either HELM's hierarchical loss at α=0.2 or SynCodonLM's synonymous-constrained softmax; species as token-type IDs over RSCU k-means clusters; AdamW at lr 2e-4 with 10% warmup, cosine decay, weight decay 0.01, dropout 0.1; 50–100M parameters; ~100B tokens.** Species conditioning is a real fork — DeCodon prepends one of 5,055 organism tokens, SynCodonLM uses token-type IDs, CodonTransformer adds organism embeddings, and both EnCodon lineages use none at all — and SynCodonLM conveniently published a V2 replicate trained *without* token-type IDs, giving a ready-made control.

## The canary matters more than the headline: 98.3% gene overlap breaks most published results

CodonBench is the paper that should set kron-dna's evaluation design, because it shows that the standard protocol measures memorisation. Its `onehot_pos` canary — encoding only (position, codon) identity, with no model at all — reaches **SynPath logistic-regression AUC 0.891 and MLP AUC 0.931 under random splits, beating every neural model**, and falls to 0.739 under leave-one-gene-out cross-validation ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.12.744371v1.full)). Under LOGO-CV the neural models land at **0.507–0.564**, below the position prior, and **ESM-2 falls from 0.685 to 0.507, essentially chance**. The mechanism is simple: **gene overlap between train and test is 98.3% under an 80/20 split and zero across all 240 LOGO folds**. LoRA fine-tuning drops **15.9–28.6 points** when splits become gene-stratified. Zero-shot log-likelihood-ratio AUC on synonymous pathogenicity is **0.484–0.526**, which directly contradicts the headline synonymous-variant claims of CodonFM/EnCodon — though the two use different ClinVar snapshots, matching schemes and metrics, so the conflict should be flagged rather than adjudicated. CodonBench also shows the familiar "deeper probes help" signature is an artifact (**+11.7 to +19.3 points from linear to MLP under random splits, −3.9 to +3.1 under LOGO-CV**), that a reported +4.7-point gain existed only with scikit-learn's `MLPClassifier` defaults and vanished with a matched PyTorch MLP, and that selecting the best epoch by test R² manufactures gains of **+6.4 to +23.7 points on mRFP expression that validation-based early stopping reverses to −37.0 to −10.8**.

The second credibility requirement is simple baselines, and here two independent 2026 groups converge. Roche found that in **5 of 9 tasks, simpler k-mer or GC-based models were about as predictive** as foundation-model embeddings, that k-mer + MLM embeddings beat MLM alone on all tasks except protein abundance, and that on a 24-sequence zero-shot set **GC content correlated 0.597 and CAI 0.423** with abundance ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.11.744178v1.full)). On the genome side, Karpinsky et al. found pretraining lifts DNABERT-2 from **45.97 to 66.88 mean MCC×100 on GUE but makes no difference at all for CNNs** (ConvNova 61.08 pretrained vs 61.30 random), and that BPE tokenization *hurts* CNNs badly (U-Net 57.82 → 34.45) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30140v1)). Tang and Koo found probing pretrained genomic LM representations gives no substantial advantage over conventional ML on one-hot sequences across six functional-genomics tasks ([Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-025-03674-8)), DART-Eval found ab initio CNNs match or beat most DNA LMs on cell-type-specific elements and that DNA LMs "perform particularly poorly on counterfactual prediction tasks" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12340898/)), and the random-initialisation study across 7 models and 52 tasks found randomly initialised counterparts give "surprisingly strong baselines" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05531v1)).

The evaluation plan that follows is specific. Report **bits per codon on held-out species and held-out gene families**, split by GC stratum. Run the **CodonBERT seven tasks** and the **SynCodonLM seven DNA-level-sensitive datasets** (frozen encoders, mean pooling, linear head, five-fold CV over 20–50 seeds, paired t-tests) for comparability, but report them **under CodonBench's LOGO-CV with the `onehot_pos` canary in every table**, alongside k-mer-frequency, codon-usage-bias and GC-content baselines, a **randomly initialised KronLSTM twin** at the same fine-tuning budget, and **ESM-2 on the translated protein**. Expect to lose to ESM-2 on missense — EnCodon is "second best to the ESM2 models" there ([NVIDIA preprint](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dbr/assets/data/manuscripts/nv-codonfm-preprint.pdf)) — and pitch the model on synonymous variation, translation efficiency and expression instead, where protein LMs are structurally blind. Add two diagnostics no one has built: a **DNA needle-in-haystack and copy test** (a planted repeat at distance d in random DNA) to measure whether the delta rule's targeted overwrite actually buys associative recall on genomic sequence, and **separate loss reporting on repeat-masked positions**, since a delta-rule model can reach low perplexity largely by copying repeats — which would help nothing downstream. Finally, re-run the baselines yourself under one harness rather than copying numbers: DNALongBench eQTL AUROCs for identical models differ by 0.3 between papers (official tables put Caduceus-PH at 0.566 while JanusDNA reports 0.842 for the same model), and the DNABERT-2 appendix records that the official HyenaDNA checkpoint did not reproduce its published results.

## Conclusion

The strategic read is that kron-dna should not try to be a general DNA foundation model. On whole genomes the 1–10M recurrent models have converged to within 0.01–0.03 of each other on saturated suites, expert supervised models still beat every DNA LM on all five DNALongBench tasks, and one-hot CNNs are competitive or better across most regulatory work — so another NT-benchmark row buys nothing. On coding DNA the picture inverts: the receptive field the task actually needs is about 17 codons, the corpora are open and well-documented, the compute envelope is four A100-weeks, three of the four strongest 2026 results came from loss-function changes rather than scale, and the entire delta-rule family is unrepresented. The combination of a bidirectional gated delta-rule backbone, a synonymy-aware objective and gene-held-out evaluation is a defensible, novel and affordable paper; the combination of the same backbone and hg38 masked language modelling is not.

The sharpest open question is whether the delta rule's distinguishing property — targeted erase-then-write rather than additive accumulation — actually matters for biological sequence. The general-LM evidence that it improves associative recall is suggestive but caps out at 4–8k, and nobody has connected synthetic recall scores to any genomic downstream result. That makes the honest version of kron-dna's claim narrow and testable: at matched parameters and matched corpus against CodonMamba's BiMamba, does a gated delta-rule recurrence do better, and is the difference attributable to recall on repeated or paralogous sequence? Answering that with a clean ablation, a published compute figure and a leakage-controlled evaluation would be worth more than a higher number on any existing leaderboard — and it is the one experiment the whole literature is currently missing.
